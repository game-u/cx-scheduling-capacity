ABOUT THIS DASHBOARD

This is a predictive staffing model for GameU's CX Scheduling team. It answers two questions: Is our team appropriately staffed for the weekly workload we're carrying? and Where is that workload coming from, so we know where to invest?

How it works: Every week the team handles dozens of activities — new-student intakes, PTO requests, sub-coverage, cancellations, escalations, schedule changes, etc. Each activity has a complexity weight based on how much coordinator time it actually takes. The model sums weighted activities per week and divides by team headcount, giving one number: weighted load per coordinator. The capacity threshold below is the line beyond which load exceeds what headcount and process can sustainably absorb.

How to read the rest of the page: green = within capacity; yellow = approaching limit; red = over capacity. Built Q1 2026 in partnership with Ops; weights are tunable and calibrated against 12 weeks of historical activity. The page refreshes twice daily.

What to do with this. Since Student Management dominates, process automation on billing changes (add/drop hours), cancellation handling, and family-facing makeup confirmations produces the biggest leverage. In any week where a bucket's load exceeds 1.2x its average, mark that bucket Yellow in the Capacity Signals tab - the model will flag the week as stressed and recalibrate the staffing index.

Key takeaways

  • No weekly capacity signals have been logged yet. Fallback threshold recommendation is 354 (median observed load). True calibration needs weekly entries in the Capacity Signals tab - once the team reports Red on any signal (or 3+ Yellows), that week's load becomes the staffing index.
  • Demand is rising: weekly workload is up +25% from Week 1 to Week 11 — per-coordinator load only fell (median 417) because coordinator headcount rose from 6 to 9 over the window and absorbed the growth. This is not steady state — with about 101 new students projected in the next 30 days, plan capacity against the demand trend, not the flattened per-coordinator line.
  • Load by bucket: Student Management (58%), Instructor Ops (40%), New Student (2%). Student Management drives the majority of the weighted load - process improvements there move the needle most.
  • Week-to-week variance is only 12% - load is predictable, so staffing to the average is reasonable.
  • 10-day SLO compliance: per the March 19 doc, new-student intakes are supposed to reach Done within 10 days of creation. 36 of 51 active pipeline records (71%) are already past that window, and 41% of all intakes completed in the last 8 weeks took longer than 10 days. This is a promise the team is not able to consistently keep - a leading indicator that the intake pipeline needs capacity investment or workflow redesign, independent of what load/coordinator says.
  • Emergency Callout vs Advance Request: across the window, Emergency Callouts fail at 24% (175 of 721) while Advance Requests fail at 18% (329 of 1817). The gap reveals where capacity actually breaks - last-minute coverage, not the planned-ahead kind. Improvements in the on-call/sub-pool layer move the Emergency Callout number; advance-notice PTO policies barely move the Advance Request number because that side is mostly working.
  • Student pipeline: 1909 active students. Historical rate is 23.6 new students/week, or ~101/month projected - that's the number to plan capacity against. Only 30 are already confirmed in XP for the next 30 days because bookings land ~2-3 weeks ahead of first class, so XP systematically undercounts the forward pipeline. Use the projection for capacity planning; use the XP confirmed count for next-2-weeks workload.
  • Detection caveat: Schedule Changes are currently undercounted (requires additional logic for permanent-series modifications). Returning Students and Parent Escalations are 0 because they live outside XP. Adjust the Excel weights if you want these to carry more inferred load.
