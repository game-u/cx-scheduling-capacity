ABOUT THIS DASHBOARD

This is a predictive staffing model for GameU's CX Scheduling team. It answers two questions: Is our team appropriately staffed for the weekly workload we're carrying? and Where is that workload coming from, so we know where to invest?

How it works: Every week the team handles dozens of activities — new-student intakes, PTO requests, sub-coverage, cancellations, escalations, schedule changes, etc. Each activity has a complexity weight based on how much coordinator time it actually takes. The model sums weighted activities per week and divides by the team's capacity in full-load equivalents, giving one number: weighted load per coordinator. The capacity threshold below is the line beyond which load exceeds what headcount and process can sustainably absorb.

Changed 19 September 2026 — numbers on this page stepped up about 13%. Nothing about the workload changed. The model used to divide by headcount (9). It now divides by the team's capacity in full-load equivalents (7.95), because four coordinators deliberately carry a smaller student load: Katelyn runs instructor PTO, and Margaret, Taylor and Amelia supervise. A week that read 366 before reads about 414 now.

How to read the rest of the page: green = within capacity; yellow = approaching limit; red = over capacity. Built Q1 2026 in partnership with Ops; weights are tunable and calibrated against 12 weeks of historical activity. The page refreshes twice daily.

Week-over-week movement. Student Management is stable (avg 2017/wk, variance 13%). Instructor Ops is stable (avg 1362/wk, variance 5%). New Student is stable (avg 56/wk, variance 53%). The chart above shows each bucket's weighted load per week - pay attention to divergence: if one bucket climbs while another stays flat, that's where the team's work is actually shifting.

Volatility. New Student swings most week-to-week (53% variance vs. mean), while Instructor Ops is the most predictable (5%). High-variance buckets need a flex/on-call layer or buffer capacity; stable buckets can be staffed to the average.

What to do with this. Since Student Management dominates, process automation on billing changes (add/drop hours), cancellation handling, and family-facing makeup confirmations produces the biggest leverage. In any week where a bucket's load exceeds 1.2x its average, mark that bucket Yellow in the Capacity Signals tab - the model will flag the week as stressed and recalibrate the staffing index.

Key takeaways

  • No weekly capacity signals have been logged yet. Fallback threshold recommendation is 334 (median observed load). True calibration needs weekly entries in the Capacity Signals tab - once the team reports Red on any signal (or 3+ Yellows), that week's load becomes the staffing index.
  • Demand is roughly flat over the measured weeks (weekly workload +5%; per-coordinator load median 393). But about 90 new students are projected in the next 30 days — watch the pipeline rather than assuming steady state.
  • Load by bucket: Student Management (59%), Instructor Ops (40%), New Student (2%). Student Management drives the majority of the weighted load - process improvements there move the needle most.
  • Week-to-week variance is only 8% - load is predictable, so staffing to the average is reasonable.
  • 10-day SLO compliance: per the March 19 doc, new-student intakes are supposed to reach Done within 10 days of creation. 14 of 14 active pipeline records (100%) are already past that window, and 41% of all intakes completed in the last 8 weeks took longer than 10 days. This is a promise the team is not able to consistently keep - a leading indicator that the intake pipeline needs capacity investment or workflow redesign, independent of what load/coordinator says.
  • Emergency Callout vs Advance Request: across the window, Emergency Callouts fail at 30% (219 of 738) while Advance Requests fail at 9% (326 of 3702). The gap reveals where capacity actually breaks - last-minute coverage, not the planned-ahead kind. Improvements in the on-call/sub-pool layer move the Emergency Callout number; advance-notice PTO policies barely move the Advance Request number because that side is mostly working.
  • Student pipeline: 1962 active students. Historical rate is 20.9 new students/week, or ~90/month projected - that's the number to plan capacity against. Only 22 are already confirmed in XP for the next 30 days because bookings land ~2-3 weeks ahead of first class, so XP systematically undercounts the forward pipeline. Use the projection for capacity planning; use the XP confirmed count for next-2-weeks workload.
  • Detection caveat: Schedule Changes are currently undercounted (requires additional logic for permanent-series modifications). Returning Students and Parent Escalations are 0 because they live outside XP. Adjust the Excel weights if you want these to carry more inferred load.
