ABOUT THIS DASHBOARD

This is a predictive staffing model for GameU's CX Scheduling team. It answers two questions: Is our team appropriately staffed for the weekly workload we're carrying? and Where is that workload coming from, so we know where to invest?

How it works: Every week the team handles dozens of activities — new-student intakes, PTO requests, sub-coverage, cancellations, escalations, schedule changes, etc. Each activity has a complexity weight based on how much coordinator time it actually takes. The model sums weighted activities per week and divides by team headcount, giving one number: weighted load per coordinator. The capacity threshold below is the line beyond which load exceeds what headcount and process can sustainably absorb.

How to read the rest of the page: green = within capacity; yellow = approaching limit; red = over capacity. Built Q1 2026 in partnership with Ops; weights are tunable and calibrated against 12 weeks of historical activity. The page refreshes twice daily.

Volatility. New Student swings most week-to-week (25% variance vs. mean), while Student Management is the most predictable (9%). High-variance buckets need a flex/on-call layer or buffer capacity; stable buckets can be staffed to the average.

What to do with this. Since Student Management dominates, process automation on billing changes (add/drop hours), cancellation handling, and family-facing makeup confirmations produces the biggest leverage. In any week where a bucket's load exceeds 1.2x its average, mark that bucket Yellow in the Capacity Signals tab - the model will flag the week as stressed and recalibrate the staffing index.

Key takeaways

  • Where the capacity ceiling comes from: the team first self-reported stress in Week 1 at a load of 401.8 per coordinator. We apply a 15% buffer below that point as the team's sustainable-load ceiling (342). The Excel model is currently set to 341 — the number you'll see used on the chart and in the status pill above.
  • Demand is rising: weekly workload is up +23% from Week 1 to Week 11 — per-coordinator load only held roughly flat (median 526) because coordinator headcount rose from 7 to 9 over the window and absorbed the growth. This is not steady state — with about 89 new students projected in the next 30 days, plan capacity against the demand trend, not the flattened per-coordinator line.
  • Load by bucket: Student Management (57%), Instructor Ops (42%), New Student (2%). Student Management drives the majority of the weighted load - process improvements there move the needle most.
  • Week-to-week variance is only 15% - load is predictable, so staffing to the average is reasonable.
  • 10-day SLO compliance: per the March 19 doc, new-student intakes are supposed to reach Done within 10 days of creation. 17 of 54 active pipeline records (31%) are already past that window, and 51% of all intakes completed in the last 8 weeks took longer than 10 days. This is a promise the team is not able to consistently keep - a leading indicator that the intake pipeline needs capacity investment or workflow redesign, independent of what load/coordinator says.
  • Emergency Callout vs Advance Request: across the window, Emergency Callouts fail at 44% (335 of 765) while Advance Requests fail at 21% (398 of 1869). The gap reveals where capacity actually breaks - last-minute coverage, not the planned-ahead kind. Improvements in the on-call/sub-pool layer move the Emergency Callout number; advance-notice PTO policies barely move the Advance Request number because that side is mostly working.
  • Student pipeline: 1831 active students. Historical rate is 20.7 new students/week, or ~89/month projected - that's the number to plan capacity against. Only 26 are already confirmed in XP for the next 30 days because bookings land ~2-3 weeks ahead of first class, so XP systematically undercounts the forward pipeline. Use the projection for capacity planning; use the XP confirmed count for next-2-weeks workload.
  • Detection caveat: Schedule Changes are currently undercounted (requires additional logic for permanent-series modifications). Returning Students and Parent Escalations are 0 because they live outside XP. Adjust the Excel weights if you want these to carry more inferred load.
